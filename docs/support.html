A plan vs actual bar chart for Power BI. It writes the variation % between bars by itself and, with your plan, budget, forecast or last year’s figure, draws a marker where each bar should have been. The free version compares against the previous bar, with small multiples, both orientations and style templates. Pro adds the three reference measures, absolute variance, up to 100 panels, analytical lines with a trend line and a reference band, value labels and per-bar colours with conditional formatting (fx).

Quick Start

  1. Add Bar Chart with Variation % to the page. With no fields it shows a start screen with these steps and links to this guide and the video.
  2. Drag a date or category field to Axis and a measure to Values. The variation % appears between the bars.
  3. Optional: a field in Small Multiple for one panel per region, product or team.
  4. Pro: drag your plan to Plan / Budget (or last year to Previous Year) and choose it under Comparison → Compare to.
  5. The setup guide in the corner lists the optional fields and the style templates. Sample data: SampleData_BarChartVariation.xlsx.

How to read it

Read each label as “this bar against the one before”, or, with a reference, “this bar against plan”. The marker shows where the bar should have been; the colour says whether it fell short.

Selection and interaction

  • Click a bar to filter the rest of the page; the other bars dim. Ctrl/Cmd + click adds; click the only selected bar again, or the background, to clear.
  • Highlight: when another visual filters this one, the highlighted bars are drawn over faded ones.
  • Bookmarks restore the selection. Right-click opens the Power BI context menu. A hierarchy in Axis gives drilldown from the visual header.
  • When the report author turns interactions off, clicks do not filter.

Accessibility

  • Keyboard: Tab moves between bars, Enter or Space selects, Esc clears. The tooltip opens on the focused bar.
  • Each bar has a description for screen readers with its panel, category, value and variation.
  • High contrast: the visual uses the colours of the Windows contrast theme and marks negative bars with a diagonal pattern.
  • The layout adapts to the size of the visual: the legend, the axis and the titles step aside in small tiles so the bars and their percentages stay legible.

FAQ

How do I compare actual against plan or budget?

Drag your plan measure to Plan / Budget and set Comparison → Compare to → Plan / Budget (Pro). Each bar is the actual figure, a marker shows the plan, and the variation label says by how much the bar is above or below it. The same works with Previous Year and Forecast.

Do I need a date table or DATEADD for the variation?

No. Against the previous bar, the visual calculates the variation from the values on the axis. For last year, plan or forecast you need those measures in your model, as you would for any report.

What does “Compare to: Previous bar” compare?

Each bar with the bar to its left in the current sort order. The label sits between the two bars, joined by a bracket. Sorting by value changes the neighbours, so the variation follows the new order.

Why are my years or months in the wrong order?

Power BI sends categories sorted by the measure, largest first. With Axis → Sort order → Auto the visual puts date and number axes in time order. For month names, use Sort by column on the month field in the model (by month number), or sort from the visual menu (… → Sort axis).

The last months show −100%

Those months have no data yet, so they are 0. Turn on Axis → Hide empty categories: categories that are 0 or blank in every panel are removed from the axis.

Why is a bar red when its value went up?

The negative colour marks a bar that is below what it is compared with — the previous bar, or the plan — or below zero. A month can grow against last year and still miss the plan.

Relative, absolute or both?

Comparison → Variance format: Relative (%) is free; Absolute shows the difference in the units of the measure and Both shows the two (Pro). It applies against the previous bar and against a reference. The difference uses the format of the measure.

The variation labels are small or at two heights

When bars are narrow — many categories, or several panels — the labels drop the arrows, shrink by up to 2 px and then alternate between two heights, so none is hidden. Widen the visual, use fewer panels per row, or switch to horizontal orientation.

Can the bars be horizontal?

Yes: Panel layout → Orientation → Horizontal (bars). Categories go on the left, the variation in a column on the right, and the value axis at the bottom. Long category names read better this way.

How do small multiples work?

Drag a field to Small Multiple: one panel per value, all on the same scale, so a taller bar is a larger value in every panel. Set the number of columns under Panel layout, or leave 0 for automatic. With many panels, set a minimum panel size and the visual scrolls. Free draws 3 panels; Pro up to 100.

What is the reference band?

A shaded range across the panels (Pro). With Band min and Band max at 0 it marks the typical range of your data — the middle half of the values, from the 25th to the 75th percentile. Type limits to mark your own range, such as a target corridor.

What does the trend line show?

A straight least-squares line through the bars of each panel, in the axis order (Pro). Use it on a time axis: on categories sorted by value it only restates the sort.

What is “IBCS style”?

A template with a sober look inspired by IBCS notation: dark grey bars, green and red variances, black reference markers and square corners. It is a style; the visual does not claim compliance with the IBCS standard.

Conditional formatting (fx) does not colour the bars

Power BI evaluates conditional formatting rules per bar only when there is no field in Small Multiple, as it does for native charts with a Legend field. Without Small Multiple, the rule colours each bar by its value.

I changed the bar colour and nothing happened

A conditional formatting rule on the bar colour takes precedence over the fixed colour, and the negative colour still marks bars below their comparison. Use Reset to default on the Bar settings card to clear an old rule.

Where do the default colours come from?

From the report theme: the first data colour for bars, and the theme’s bad and good colours for negative and positive. Any colour you choose replaces them.

How are numbers formatted?

With the number format of the measure from the model, dynamic format strings included, in the report’s language. Display units and decimals for the value labels are set under Data labels.

How many categories and panels can it show?

Up to 1,000 categories and 100 panels, which is what the visual asks Power BI for.

Do bookmarks keep my selection?

Bookmarks keep the bars you select and the filters of the page. Like every Power BI visual, they do not keep formatting.

Can I format it by clicking on the chart?

Yes. In Power BI Desktop with on-object interaction turned on, choose Format and click the bars, the variation labels, a panel title, an axis or the legend.

What is the "Pro preview" watermark?

While you edit a report without a licence, Pro features you turn on are drawn working under a watermark that names them, and Power BI shows its licence notice. Reading view shows the free result. Where Power BI provides no licence information (for example when you are not signed in to Power BI Desktop), the free result is shown.

How do I get Pro?

On the visual's Microsoft AppSource page, choose Buy now (not Install free). Licences are per user and managed by Microsoft; restart Power BI Desktop after the licence is assigned. See tcviz.com/licensing.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The visual makes no network requests and stores nothing outside your report.
